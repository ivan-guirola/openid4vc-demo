The token it received

One string with three parts joined by ~. Each JWT inside is three Base64url pieces joined by a dot: header, content and signature. Base64url only makes the text safe to send; anyone can read it.

            Below: who holds each key, what each party ended up with, and a forged answer
          
      
        
      
      
      

      
      
        
      
          
      Inside the token
      
          
      
            The real data from your last answer. Nothing here is encrypted: City Hall signs the card, every field is
            hidden behind a fingerprint, and your wallet signs each answer. The trip itself is protected by HTTPS.
          
      
        
      

        
      
          
      Who holds which key
      
          
      
            Both keys are ES256: an elliptic curve key pair (P-256) that signs with ECDSA over a SHA-256 hash. The private
            part signs, the public part only checks.
          
      
          
      
        
      

        
      
          
      What each party has
      
          
      
        
      

        
      
          
      Each field of the card
      
          
      
            City Hall gave every field a random salt, joined it with the name and the value, and put only the SHA-256
            fingerprint of that in the card it signed. A fingerprint cannot be turned back into the value, and the salt
            stops anyone guessing it by trying every date or a simple “true”. Open a field to follow the steps.

        What real deployments add
        
          
          
            
        • 
              The answer to the verifier can also be encrypted for it alone (OpenID4VP direct_post.jwt, a JWE
              with ECDH-ES), so only the verifier can read it, even where HTTPS ends at a proxy along the way.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              The card shows the same signature and wallet key to every verifier, so two of them could tell it is the
              same card. Wallets avoid this by holding several copies of it and using each copy once.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              The wallet keeps its private key in the phone’s secure hardware, and City Hall can revoke the card through
              a status list.

          What you just saw
          
        
            
          
          • 
            The card is yours. City Hall signs it once and is not contacted again when you show it.
          
            • 
          
          • 
            You share one fact, not the whole card. The cinema can check that fact is genuine without
            seeing the rest.
          
            • 
          
          • 
            A copy is useless. Each answer is signed by your wallet for that one request.
          
            • 
          
          • 
            Nothing is secret, everything is checkable. Signatures and fingerprints protect the card,
            not encryption.
